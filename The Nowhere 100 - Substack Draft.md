# The Nowhere 100

### Who Actually Runs the World, and Where That Power Sits

*A dated, filterable ranking of the hundred most powerful people alive, scored not by vibes but by the place or institution behind each one. It is the natural sequel to a project that spent two years measuring cities. Having ranked the stage, I went looking for the actors.*

## Why I built it

Two years ago I set out to answer a deceptively simple question: which cities have built the most complete ecosystem of infrastructure, culture, finance, sport and connectivity? That became [the Global Metro Power Rankings](https://citizenofnowhere.substack.com/p/the-global-metro-power-rankings), a sixteen-dimension score across more than four thousand metros. Then I put every sports team on the same map in [Every Metro Keeps Score](https://citizenofnowhere.substack.com/p/every-metro-keeps-score-the-sports).

The whole time, one question kept nagging. I had ranked the stage. I had not ranked the actors. Every list of "the most powerful people" I could find was a magazine editor's dinner-party guess, reshuffled once a year and defended by nobody. Power, though, is not free-floating. It sits somewhere. A president is only as powerful as the state he commands; a mayor as the metro she runs; a CEO as the company he steers; a striker as the club that pays him. Power has an address.

So I gave it one. [The Nowhere 100](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/power) scores each person by the Metro Power of the place or institution behind them, weighted by how much control they actually hold, and then it links every single name back to that place on the map. It is opinionated, transparent, dated, and unlike any other power list, it shows its work.

## How to read it

The core formula is one line: **power equals the Metro Power score of a person's jurisdiction, times an influence weight for how much of that jurisdiction they actually command.** A head of state in a full autocracy captures nearly all of their country's weight; a prime minister checked by coalitions and courts captures roughly half. On top of that sits a geopolitical multiplier for the nuclear and energy powers whose clout outruns their economy.

A few things are worth knowing before you scroll.

**Fifteen kinds of power, not one.** Heads of state share the board with central bankers, governors and mayors, judges, multilateral and alliance chiefs, corporate CEOs scored on market cap, financiers scored on assets under management, media owners, sports-governance figures, cultural icons scored on reach, sovereigns, faith leaders, and billionaires scored on net worth. [UPLOAD HERE: The Nowhere 100 - Categories.png — bar chart of how the 100 break down by category]

**A filter for the purists.** Some readers will find a footballer or a pop star on a power list absurd. Toggle to "Office and wealth" and the media, sport, culture, royalty and faith entries vanish, leaving only formal office and economic control. Both views are legitimate; the toggle just decides how wide you want the tent.

**A clock for what is about to change.** A small hourglass marks anyone facing a leadership change or election in roughly the next six months. As I write, it sits on Keir Starmer, who is resigning as the [United Kingdom](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/countries/united-kingdom) hands its premiership to Andy Burnham; on António Guterres, whose UN term ends in December; and on Mike Johnson and John Thune, whose grip depends on the November midterms.

**A date, always.** This is a snapshot, not a monument. Every entry carries an "as of" date, because power in July is not power in December.

The interesting part is not the top. Trump and Xi sit where everyone expects. The interesting part is what the model surfaces below the fold, and where it plants its flag.

**Capital outranks cabinet.** [Larry Fink](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/new-york) of BlackRock, steward of some twelve trillion dollars, lands at twelve, one spot above the Chancellor of [Germany](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/countries/germany). That is a claim, and I will defend it: the money that moves markets is a more durable lever than a coalition government that could fall next quarter.

**The Pope, but not only the Pope.** Faith is real power, so Leo XIV sits at fourteen on the reach of 1.3 billion Catholics. The harder call was that the Grand Imam of Al-Azhar, the leading authority in Sunni Islam, also belongs on the board. Representing one faith and not the others was its own kind of statement.

**Nuclear weight versus economic weight.** Kim Jong-un commands a bomb and total control of his state, yet North Korea's tiny economy drags him to the thirties. I tuned the model hard on exactly this tension, adding a coercive-power multiplier so the arsenals are not buried by GDP, and it is still the most contestable seam in the whole thing.

**Culture is power with an asterisk.** Cristiano Ronaldo and Taylor Swift share a page with heads of state, scored on planetary reach rather than any office. Roger Goodell makes it on the sheer economic mass of the NFL. If that offends you, the toggle is right there. That it is a live argument is the point.

## Beyond people: two more ways to rank the world

The same engine that ranks people ranks nations and moments.

The [Zone Zero Cup](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/sports/zone-zero-cup) is a single table of national sporting merit across fourteen pillars, modelled on the college Directors' Cup but built for countries. It weights recent achievement far more heavily than distant glory, on an eight-year half-life, so it reflects who is strong now rather than who hoarded medals a century ago. The United States leads, but switch to the per-capita or per-GDP view and the story flips to whoever punches hardest above their size.

[UPLOAD HERE: Zone Zero Cup - Top 15.png — bar chart of the top 15 sporting nations by merit score]

[The Greatest Games](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/sports/games) does the same for individual matches. Every postseason game in the NFL, NBA and MLB gets a Game Score that blends stakes, quality and how close it actually was, so a first-round upset and a Game 7 classic can sit on one ladder. Football's greatest matches join too. The 1954 Miracle of Bern still tops the board, ahead of the 1966 final at Wembley and the Maracanazo of 1950.

## The World Cup, live and self-correcting

The clearest proof that this is machinery, not a magazine, is the [2026 World Cup hub](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/teams/national#wc2026). Group tables update from the live feed every half hour. The moment a knockout tie finishes, the bracket advances the winner into the next round on the same cycle, with no editor and no redeploy. A Monte Carlo simulation, forty thousand runs, recomputes each morning to price every side's odds of reaching the semifinal, the final and the trophy.

[SCREENSHOT HERE: the Round of 16 bracket on https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/teams/national#wc2026 showing Paraguay-France and the title-odds table]

It also corrects itself in public. When Germany went out to Paraguay on penalties in the Round of 32, the model had to do two things properly: advance Paraguay, not Germany, into the correct Round of 16 slot, and strike Germany from the title odds entirely. Every knocked-out side, penalties included, loses its odds the moment the whistle blows. Each team on the bracket links straight to its national page and the metro that hosts the match.

## How often it updates

Once a week, automatically. Every Monday a single job refreshes the world's leaders, the billionaire net worths, the team valuations and the ranking itself, and publishes without triggering a rebuild. The World Cup moves faster, on the half-hour live cycle above. The editorial dials, the weights and the reach estimates, are mine to turn, and I turn them in the open. Nothing here is a black box, and nothing here waits for me to remember to update it.

## How it is built, and where it could be wrong

The full method, including every weight, lives on the [methodology page](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/methodology). National leaders scale with the V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index; billionaires convert net worth to the same scale at a discount; corporate chiefs run on market cap, financiers on assets, and the softer categories on estimated global reach, which is deliberately the most arguable input on the board.

And here is the honest caveat. The further this model travels from formal office and hard money toward reach and culture, the more it becomes an argument rather than a measurement. A model that puts a governor above most prime ministers, or a striker above a central banker, is making a case, not stating a fact. That is why the toggle exists, why the methodology is public, and why the whole thing is dated. I would rather be transparently wrong and improvable than confidently vague.
