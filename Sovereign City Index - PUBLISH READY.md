# The Sovereign City Index

**Ranking the world's planned cities by the gap between what was announced and what is actually on the ground.**

*The most expensive lie in twenty-first-century urbanism is the rendering. Saudi Arabia spent the last decade selling a 170-kilometre linear city anchored to [Tabuk](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/tabuk); in 2024 the actual buildable phase was conceded to be roughly 2.4. Country Garden announced a 700,000-resident Forest City off [Johor Bahru](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/johor-bahru); the resident count is in the low five figures and the developer has defaulted. Egypt opened a New Administrative Capital meant to relieve [Cairo](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/cairo) of its working population; the visible occupancy is a few hundred employees rotating through what was sold as a six-million-person metropolis. Each of these projects raised real money, broke real ground, and produced a real photograph. None of them has produced the city in the rendering. The Sovereign City Index is an attempt to score that gap explicitly, and to do so with the same dimensional discipline the [Global Metro Power Rankings](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org) brings to extant cities.*

## What the index measures

A "realism composite" of four dimensions, each scored 0 to 25, summing to 0 to 100. The dimensions are deliberately chosen to be observable from public reporting rather than from announcement materials, because announcement materials are the thing being measured against. The construction borrows the same discipline used for the [composite score](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/methodology#dimensions) and the [tier vocabulary](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/methodology#tiers) that anchor the live rankings, but applies it to projects that have not yet earned a place in those rankings on their own merits.

**Capital deployment ratio.** Announced spend versus disclosed actual spend, where actual spend is anchored to public-private filings, sovereign budget line items, contractor pay schedules, and credible third-party reporting. Vaporware concentrates here. A project that announces 500 billion and demonstrably spends 80 scores low on this dimension regardless of what is built; a project that announces 8 and spends 8 scores high.

**Population yield.** Verified resident headcount divided by the stated target population at the present year. Ghost cities concentrate here. The denominator is whatever the project's own published timeline says it should hit by now; the numerator is residents who actually sleep there. Construction workers and rotating government employees do not count.

**Governance autonomy.** Whether the city has the legal, regulatory, and fiscal authority to do what its plan requires. A free zone with its own visa and tax framework scores higher than a project sitting under conventional municipal control. A project under direct sovereign or military authority scores lower than one with a chartered authority and a publicly accountable governance board, because direct sovereign authority correlates with downside protection rather than upside execution.

**Milestone fidelity.** The fraction of publicly announced milestones met on the originally stated timeline, with explicit credit for projects that revised their targets honestly before the market revised them involuntarily. The Line lost almost all its milestone-fidelity score in 2024 not because it cannot build a 2.4-kilometre segment, but because the 170-kilometre version was the publicly stated milestone and the revision was forced by reality rather than chosen as a planning decision.

The composite is the sum. Brasília, scored as a 1960 calibration case rather than a present-day index entry, sits at roughly 85; the canonical successful sovereign city. Putrajaya at 1995 launch sits in the high 60s. The Line in 2026 sits in the low teens. The point of the dimension construction is that the same city can carry a strong score on capital deployment and a poor score on population yield, and that pattern is itself the story.

A note on what this ranking is and is not. It is not a verdict on whether the project will eventually succeed; Songdo at twenty years out has had ample opportunity to fall further than it has. It is not a verdict on whether the underlying vision was worth pursuing; Nusantara is among the most geopolitically rational capital-relocation projects on this list and still scores in the bottom four because the realism dimensions do not yet have material under them. The score is a snapshot of the announcement-to-reality gap as it stands in 2026.

## Six exemplars

### [Astana / Nur-Sultan](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/astana)

The textbook case at the top of the index. Kazakhstan's capital relocation from [Almaty](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/almaty) was announced in 1994, designated in 1997, and operational by 1998. The headline numbers are unusual for a planned-city project: target population of one million by 2020, actual population approximately 1.4 million; announced capital spend of roughly 30 billion dollars, demonstrably deployed; Expo 2017 hosted on the announced site, on the announced timeline. The scoring weakness is governance autonomy; the city is a sovereign capital under a centralised regime, which earns the structural-functioning bonus on capital deployment but caps the autonomy dimension. Almaty, separately, carries the [Emerging Standout](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/badges/emerging-standout) badge in the live rankings, and the contrast between the two cities is exactly the lesson Astana teaches: the most reliable way to build a planned city is to relocate a functioning state apparatus into it on a forced timeline. The capital-relocation cases on this list outperform the speculative-development cases by a wide and consistent margin.

### Putrajaya (satellite of [Kuala Lumpur](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/kuala-lumpur))

The mid-table calibration case. Malaysia's federal administrative capital was announced in 1995, broke ground in 1996, and absorbed the federal government's relocation from Kuala Lumpur between 1999 and 2003. Where Putrajaya loses points is on population yield. The city was sold as a 320,000-resident administrative metropolis by 2010; the actual count in 2024 sits closer to 110,000. Where it gains points is on milestone fidelity, capital deployment honesty, and governance: the Federal Territory designation gave Putrajaya the autonomy it needed to function as a working capital from day one, and the spending profile tracked the plan. Putrajaya is what a sovereign city looks like when the government does not let its own marketing inflate the resident-count target into something the underlying labor market cannot absorb. Most cities on this list would benefit from the Putrajaya discount of dividing announcement-day population claims by three before scoring credibility.

### Songdo International Business District (within [Greater Seoul](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/seoul))

The mid-table partial-success case. Songdo was announced in 2003 as a smart-city tabula rasa anchoring a Free Economic Zone inside Incheon, with a target of 350,000 residents and a multinational professional class that would treat Northeast Asia's logistical centroid as a base of operations. Twenty-three years on, the resident count is roughly 210,000 and the multinational professional class arrived in materially smaller numbers than the IFEZ plan called for. What Songdo demonstrates is the dimension that does not show up in the four-axis scoring: a planned city's anchor strategy has to survive contact with the macroeconomic environment. The Free Economic Zone framework was the right autonomy posture; the smart-city infrastructure works; the Cisco partnership was a credible anchor in 2003. None of that fully compensated for the gravitational pull of [Seoul](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/seoul) thirty kilometres away, the eighth-ranked metro in the live composite. Songdo is the case the next ten projects on this list should study.

### New Administrative Capital (satellite of [Cairo](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/cairo))

The high-spend, low-yield case. Announced in 2015, partly opened in 2023, the NAC is intended to relocate the Egyptian government and absorb six and a half million long-term residents into the desert east of [Cairo](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/cairo). The capital deployment ratio is the strongest dimension of the index for the project: roughly 58 billion dollars announced, with demonstrable deployment in infrastructure, government office construction, the central business district towers, and the Iconic Tower (Africa's tallest, the kind of asset the live rankings classify under the [Skyline City](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/badges/skyline-city) badge). The weakness is governance autonomy. The Administrative Capital for Urban Development (ACUD) holding company is majority-owned by the Egyptian military, which produces a project that can deploy capital at scale but cannot easily attract the private-sector commercial occupancy the city's economic case depends on. Population yield in the present year is well under five percent of the long-term target, and the relocated workforce is mostly rotating government rather than resident. The NAC is the index's clearest illustration that sovereign autonomy and project autonomy point in opposite directions, and that a project run by the parent state's most powerful unitary actor is harder to populate, not easier.

### [Nusantara](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/nusantara)

The early-phase case. Indonesia's planned new capital on East Kalimantan, sited between [Balikpapan](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/balikpapan) and [Samarinda](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/samarinda) and intended to relieve [Jakarta](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/jakarta), was announced in 2019, legislated in 2022, and ceremonially inaugurated for Independence Day 2024. The Otorita Ibu Kota Nusantara (OIKN) is a credibly-structured authority with proper enabling legislation and a governance design closer to Putrajaya's than to the NAC's. The realism gap is everywhere else. Capital deployment is running below the announced 33 billion dollar profile and the foreign investment that the original plan assumed never materialised. The Prabowo administration in late 2024 quietly began revising the timeline, which under this index's scoring rule preserves milestone fidelity points (an honest revision before market forces it). Resident population at the present year is in the hundreds rather than the millions targeted by 2045, and Nusantara already appears in the live rankings at composite 0.6 — a score that captures exactly how little material the city currently has under it. Nusantara could plausibly climb this index over the next ten years; it could equally plausibly become this list's third-largest cautionary tale. The honest read is that it has not yet generated enough material to score high on any dimension other than governance design.

### The Line / NEOM (anchored to [Tabuk](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/tabuk))

The bottom-of-index canonical case. NEOM was announced in 2017 as a 500-billion-dollar special economic zone in northwest Saudi Arabia, sited along the Tabuk coast and pitched as a counterweight to the gravitational pull of [Riyadh](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/riyadh) and [Jeddah](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/jeddah). The Line was announced in 2021 as a 170-kilometre, 9-million-resident linear city to be substantially complete by 2030. The 2024 admission that the buildable phase one was being scaled to roughly 2.4 kilometres collapsed the project's milestone fidelity score by an order of magnitude. The other dimensions reflect the same reality. Capital deployment is running well below the announced profile despite headline-level investment by the Public Investment Fund. Resident population is a few dozen executives and somewhat larger numbers of construction workers in temporary accommodation. Sindalah, the luxury island component, did open in 2024 on a credible timeline and a credible population scale, which is the only place the project gains points on this index. Trojena, the ski resort hosting the 2029 Asian Winter Games, has a chance to gain similar credit if it delivers on time. The structural problem with The Line is that the Crown Prince's direct authority is the dimension protecting the project from cancellation and the dimension preventing it from being honestly re-scoped. Authority and accountability are not a substitute for each other in planned-city construction.

## What the index actually predicts

Three things, none of them the things planned-city marketing materials usually emphasise.

First, capital-relocation projects systematically outperform speculative-development projects on this index, and the gap is large enough to be the index's headline finding. [Astana](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/astana), Putrajaya, and [Naypyidaw](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/rankings/naypyidaw) all relocated functioning state apparatus into a planned site on a forced timeline, and all three sit in the top half. Songdo, Konza, Eko Atlantic, Forest City, and The Line are speculative-development plays where the city has to attract its own population on its own merits, and all five sit in the bottom half. The single most reliable predictor of a planned city succeeding is whether it has a sovereign function being relocated into it. The market does not on its own fill an empty city, a pattern the live rankings reinforce: the metros at the top of the [composite](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org) almost universally combine a sovereign or quasi-sovereign anchor with a deep private-sector layer.

Second, the governance autonomy dimension matters less for whether a project gets built and more for whether it can be honestly re-scoped when the original plan does not survive contact with reality. Putrajaya could quietly accept that 110,000 was the actual sustainable population and move on; The Line could not concede the 170-kilometre target until external pressure forced the admission. The cities with chartered authorities and accountable governance boards close the announcement-to-reality gap incrementally over time. The cities under direct sovereign authority either deliver the original plan or carry the reputational debt of the deferred admission. Both outcomes are observable on this list.

Third, the index suggests a category of urbanism that the conventional "city rankings" debate systematically under-discusses: the planned city as a deliberately scaled-down version of its own announcement. The most successful cases on this list are the ones whose ten-year residents represent thirty to fifty percent of the original target. The least successful are the ones still claiming the original target. Modesty is structural protection in this category. The next round of planned-city announcements would benefit from announcing a target at the realism-discounted level rather than at the marketing-grade aspiration level. The same discipline shows up in the rankings' [Skyline City](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/badges/skyline-city) badge, which separates metros that built height into a diversified urban economy from those that built height instead of one.

The Sovereign City Index is the [Academic Gravity Wells](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/badges/academic-gravity-wells) move applied to a different category: take a class of cities the conventional rankings under-frame, build a single-purpose lens that surfaces what they actually have in common, and rank them on the dimension that the announcement copy obscures. The realism gap is the dimension. The same approach produced the [Greying Power](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/badges/greying-power) badge for cities that have stopped growing and started getting a few things right, and the [Cosmopolitan Capital](https://rankings.citizenofnowhere.org/badges/cosmopolitan-capital) badge for capitals whose cultural-economic mix punches above their headline rank. The Sovereign City Index will refresh annually, and the cohort will expand as new projects clear the inclusion thresholds.

## Methodology and sources

Capital figures are stated as announced versus demonstrably deployed, with deployment anchored to project-sponsor filings, sovereign budget releases, and credible third-party reporting where the sponsor publishes nothing project-specific. Population figures are stated against the project's own original-timeline target where one exists, divided by the most recent independent or government-sourced resident count. Where deployment or resident counts are not credibly disclosed, the relevant sub-dimension was demoted explicitly in scoring; the projects affected are flagged below.

**Astana / Nur-Sultan.** Capital: cumulative FDI cycles tracked via the Office of the Prime Minister of Kazakhstan ([summary](https://www.primeminister.kz/en/news/reviews/investment-attraction-large-scale-construction-and-international-recognition-how-the-main-city-of-kazakhstan-developed)). Population: ~1.65M as of 1 March 2026, [Bureau of National Statistics, Republic of Kazakhstan](https://stat.gov.kz/en/region/astana/).

**Putrajaya.** Capital: ~RM33.29B (~US$8.1B) deployed; +RM4B Kota Madani megaproject 2025, [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/06/27/explained-the-rm4b-kota-madani-megaproject-set-to-transform-putrajayas-landscape/181808). Population: ~120k Q1 2024, [Department of Statistics Malaysia, district estimates](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/current-population-estimates-by-administrative-district-).

**Modi'in.** Capital: no consolidated lifetime construction tally is published; sub-dimension demoted on this basis. Project context, [Safdie Architects](https://www.safdiearchitects.com/projects/new-city-of-modiin). Population: 100,052 (2024), [City Population, drawing on Israel CBS](https://www.citypopulation.de/en/israel/admin/hamerkaz/1200__modiin_makkabbim_reut/).

**Naypyidaw.** Capital: ~US$4B widely cited; junta-era opacity means this is best-available rather than auditable; sub-dimension demoted accordingly. Context, [Naypyidaw on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw). Population: ~924,608 (city) / ~1.16M (Union Territory), [City Population, Myanmar Department of Population data](https://www.citypopulation.de/en/myanmar/mun/admin/15__naypyitaw/).

**Songdo IBD.** Capital: ~US$30B of announced ~US$40B deployed; 19.5M sq ft built across 106 LEED-certified buildings, [Gale International](http://www.galeintl.com/project/songdo-international-business-district/). Population: 212,085 (Nov 2024), [NamuWiki, citing Incheon city statistics](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EC%86%A1%EB%8F%84%EA%B5%AD%EC%A0%9C%EB%8F%84%EC%8B%9C). Note: the 350,000 denominator used here is the IFEZ master-plan figure; against the developer's narrower 65,000 number the project would score materially higher on population yield.

**Diamniadio Lake City.** Capital: ~US$2B announced, deployment small relative to announcement, [Megatrends Afrika Policy Brief 06, October 2022](https://www.megatrends-afrika.de/assets/afrika/publications/policybrief/Schlimmer_Megatrends_policy_brief_Diamniadio_final_191022.pdf). Population: no current independent resident count published; sub-dimension demoted. Context, [Diamniadio Lake City on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamniadio_Lake_City).

**Eko Atlantic.** Capital: ~US$6B announced; >80% land reclamation complete; corporate occupancy ramping with FirstBank, MTN, Shell, US Consulate, [African Development Bank ESIA/ESMP Summary](https://www.afdb.org/fileadmin/uploads/afdb/Documents/Environmental-and-Social-Assessments/Nigeria_Eko_Atlantic_ESIA_ESMP_Summary.pdf). Population: no resident count published by Eko Atlantic Holdings or Lagos State; sub-dimension demoted. Context, [Eko Atlantic on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eko_Atlantic).

**New Administrative Capital (Egypt).** Capital: ~US$58B announced, ~LE500B (~US$11B) Phase 1 deployed, [African Business, July 2025](https://african.business/2025/07/trade-investment/can-egypts-new-administrative-capital-defy-the-sceptics). Population: ~1,500 families (~5,000–7,500 residents) plus 48,000 commuting government employees as of mid-2025, same source. Corroborating analysis, [POMED report on the NAC](https://mideastdc.org/publication/pomed-report-looks-at-al-sisis-58-billion-new-capital-city-a-bubble-about-to-burst/).

**Nusantara.** Capital: $33B announced; ~Rp 65.3T (~$4B) realised investment as of September 2025; central state allocation cut from £2B (2024) to £700M (2025) to £300M (2026), [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/investors-hold-back-as-plans-for-indonesias-future-capital-scaled-down/). Population: ~147,430 enumerated by BPS in late 2025 (largely pre-existing residents of the East Kalimantan area, not new IKN arrivals), [BPS Indonesia, 18 December 2025](https://www.bps.go.id/en/news/2025/12/18/827/bps-bappenas-oikn-luncurkan-hasil-pendataan-penduduk-ikn.html).

**Konza Technopolis.** Capital: $14.5B announced; KSh 99.4B (~US$770M) cumulative public+private investment as of 2025; only 33 of 515 surveyed parcels leased and 9 developed by end of 2024, [Kenyan Wallstreet](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/billions-in-idle-tech-undermine-konza-smart-citys-ambitions). Population: no resident count published; sub-dimension demoted. Context, [Konza Technopolis on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konza_Technopolis).

**The Line / NEOM.** Capital: $500B–$1.5T announced; PIF $8B writedown end-2024; 2025 spending cut 20–60% across portfolio; ~$16.2B in contracts awarded 2023; total deployed not disclosed at project level. PIF publishes no auditable cumulative spend figure for NEOM; this score rests on announced numbers and one-off contract disclosures and the capital sub-dimension is demoted on that basis, [Bloomberg, 5 April 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-05/saudis-scale-back-ambition-for-1-5-trillion-desert-project-neom). Population and milestone scaling: original 1.5M residents in The Line by 2030 vs scaled-back 5km / sub-300k by 2030, with roughly 2.4km foundation work on the ground as of March 2026 and construction reportedly suspended September 2025, [New Civil Engineer, 8 April 2024](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/latest/neom-plans-for-saudi-arabias-linear-city-cut-from-170km-to-2-4km-08-04-2024/).

**Forest City.** Capital: $100B announced (Country Garden); developer in technical default August 2023, $11B offshore bond cross-defaults October 2023, [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3233246/malaysias-country-garden-forest-city-us100-billion-ghost-town-caught-china-property-giants-woes). Population: 700,000 target vs ~9,000 (developer claim) / ~2,000 (independent reporting) actual residents in 2024, [Foreign Policy, 18 March 2024](https://foreignpolicy.com/2024/03/18/malaysia-china-real-estate-countrygarden-forestcity/).
