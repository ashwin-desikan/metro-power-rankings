# LinkedIn playbook

Operating rules for distributing Citizen of Nowhere pieces on LinkedIn. Internal document, gitignored.

LinkedIn is the single best fit for the existing professional graph and the most underbuilt distribution surface for analytical cities and sports content right now. Conversion shape is different from Reddit: lower viral ceiling, much higher quality of inbound. Consulting prospects, podcast invites, conference speaking, and journalist outreach all come through here, not Reddit.

The Top Teams pieces and the cities-as-organizations framing perform particularly well on LinkedIn. Sports business, urban economics, and operating-model analogies are the three lenses that resonate.

## Three post archetypes

Every LinkedIn post should fit one of these three shapes. Mixing archetypes within a post dilutes engagement on every dimension.

### 1. The single-chart insight post

**Shape:** one chart (image), six to twelve short paragraphs of analysis, no outbound link in the body, link in the first comment.

**When to use:** the day after a Substack long-form goes up. The chart is already built (it is the OG card or a derivative). The paragraphs are a tightened version of the Substack hook plus the most quotable two or three findings.

**Length:** target 1,200 to 1,800 characters in the body. LinkedIn truncates at 1,300 unless the reader clicks "see more"; the truncation point should land on a hook that makes them click.

**Example structure for the AGW post:**

> If a Fortune 500 company derived 90 percent of revenue from a single product line, we would call that concentration risk and the board would demand a diversification plan.
>
> Some cities are built that way.
>
> [chart of Tier A skyline cities, or the AGW Tier A bar chart]
>
> In Wageningen, Uppsala, Leiden, and Göttingen, a single university accounts for 90 percent of what the city is in any composite economic sense. These institutions are 300 to 550 years old. They predate the modern Dutch and Swedish states.
>
> The interesting question is not whether these cities are fragile in the company-town sense. The question is whether a 500-year-old institution that has survived plagues, wars, occupations, and reformations is closer to the most fragile entity on the map, or the most antifragile.
>
> I mapped the 102 metros where universities dominate the composite. The pattern surprised me. The post lays out the structural reasons in three or four paragraphs.
>
> Full piece in the comments.

**Why this works:** the concentration-risk frame is corporate-strategy language the audience speaks fluently. The chart anchors the visual claim. The withheld answer in paragraph three drives the click.

### 2. The contrarian-take post

**Shape:** 200 to 400 words taking an unintuitive position from the data. Structured as opinion, not analysis. Ends with a question to drive comments.

**When to use:** the second weekly post (Thursday). Not derivative of the Tuesday Substack; pulled from a different anomaly or a contested call from Top Teams.

**Length:** 800 to 1,400 characters. Tighter than the chart post.

**Example structure:**

> Here is an unpopular take from the global metro data: London is overrated.
>
> Not in absolute terms. London is rank 2 globally, behind only New York. The case is unimpeachable on every dimension I measure.
>
> Overrated *relative to the discourse*. The professional class in London talks about London as if it is the apex of European urbanism. The data says London punches 14.5 times above its population weight. That is extraordinary. It is also the same multiple as Monaco at 11.2x, or San Francisco-San Jose at 17.6x.
>
> Three Anglo-financial outliers in the same band tells a story about the financialization of cities, not about London specifically.
>
> The cities that are genuinely surprising on this measure are the small Swiss outliers. Geneva, Basel, Lausanne. Each within commuting distance of the others. Each at 8x or higher. A polycentric financial archipelago that is collectively roughly equivalent to one mid-sized world city, by score, with a fraction of the population.
>
> The real overrated/underrated debate in European urbanism is not London vs. Paris. It is whether the Swiss model can be replicated.
>
> Where else does this shape exist? Curious to hear thoughts.

**Why this works:** the contrarian framing gets the post past the algorithm's "more of the same" filter. The closing question generates comments, which the algorithm rewards heavily.

### 3. The carousel breakdown

**Shape:** PDF carousel of 6 to 10 slides walking through a methodology choice, a contested ranking call, or a regional deep dive. Native LinkedIn document upload (not slides converted to images individually).

**When to use:** when a Substack piece had outsized engagement and there is appetite for a deeper walkthrough. Not weekly. Aim for one carousel every 2 to 3 weeks.

**Length:** 6 to 10 slides, 1080x1080 each. Each slide should make one point, with a chart or map per slide.

**Slide-by-slide template for "Why a city is in our top 10":**

1. Cover slide: the metro name, the headline number ("New York: rank 1, score 181, tier Global Capital"), one-sentence hook
2. The composite breakdown: stacked bar showing the 16 dimensions
3. The two or three dimensions where the metro outperforms (one slide each, with a comparison metro)
4. The dimension where the metro underperforms (one slide, with the explanation)
5. The contested call (one slide, naming the contested choice and the rationale)
6. What this says about the methodology (one slide)
7. Closing slide: link to the full Substack piece (LinkedIn does not allow live links inside carousels, but the slide can repeat the URL as text)

**Why carousels work on LinkedIn:** PDF carousels currently outperform other LinkedIn formats by 3 to 5x on impressions. The format rewards depth and discourages quick scrolling. The audience self-selects for engagement.

## Posting cadence

| Day | Post | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tuesday morning (8-10am ET) | Single-chart insight post | Same-week Substack long-form |
| Thursday morning (8-10am ET) | Contrarian-take post | Different anomaly from the digest |
| As-needed (every 2-3 weeks) | Carousel breakdown | Most-engaged recent piece |

**Why these windows:** LinkedIn engagement peaks twice per day on weekdays, with the morning window (8-10am ET) outperforming the afternoon. Tuesday and Thursday are the empirical sweet spots; Monday is too cluttered by Sunday-night queue, Friday declines through the day, and weekends are dead for B2B-adjacent content.

**Repurposing rule:** every Substack long-form yields exactly one LinkedIn post within 48 hours of publish. Contested-call pieces from Top Teams are pre-mapped to LinkedIn posts because the format suits the audience: London (Arsenal vs Chelsea), Milan (Inter vs AC), Istanbul (the three-way), Glasgow (Old Firm), Belgrade (Red Star vs Partizan), Prague (Sparta vs Slavia), Belo Horizonte (Cruzeiro vs Atletico Mineiro).

## Hashtag discipline

3 to 5 hashtags maximum. Mix one broad with two niche.

**Broad (pick one):** #SportsBusiness, #UrbanEconomics, #Geography, #DataAnalysis, #CityRankings

**Niche (pick two to four):** #SportsAnalytics, #UrbanPlanning, #MetroEconomics, #GlobalCities, #SportsGeography, #CityComparison, #InfrastructureAnalysis, #UniversityTowns (when relevant), #SkylineEconomy (when relevant)

Avoid: #Innovation, #Leadership, #Marketing — too generic, no targeted reach. #Future, #Trends — same.

## Comment strategy

**OP comment within 5 minutes of post going live:** the canonical URL with one-line context. Format example:

> Full piece with the 102-metro CSV download: [URL]. Methodology link in the post.

**Engagement priority for the first hour:**

1. Reply to every comment within 60 minutes. LinkedIn's algorithm weights early commenter engagement very heavily.
2. Pin the substantive comment if one emerges that helps frame the post.
3. Tag specific people who are likely to engage *only if you have a working relationship*. Tagging cold is poor form on LinkedIn.

**Engagement priority for the next 24 hours:**

1. Substantive replies to every new comment.
2. If the post is performing well, a follow-up comment that adds a new finding or chart. This re-surfaces the post in the feed of people who already engaged.

**What not to do:**

- The auto-replier comment trick ("commenting for reach"). Algorithm penalizes this and audience notices.
- Generic emoji reactions to commenters. Words only.
- Cross-posting links to the Substack inside replies to other people's unrelated posts. Spam behavior.

## What success looks like, per post

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Impressions in first 60 minutes | 2,000+ |
| Impressions at 24 hours | 8,000-15,000 for an analytical post |
| Comments | 5+ substantive (not "great post" emoji) |
| Profile visits | Tracked via LinkedIn analytics; aim for 50+ per post |
| Newsletter follows from the post | 20-50 per high-performing post |
| Direct messages from new contacts | 1-3 per high-performing post is healthy |

A LinkedIn post that hits 50,000+ impressions almost always converts to inbound contact: podcast invite, conference invitation, journalist quote request, or consulting inquiry. Track these in `/internal/inbound_log.md` (does not exist yet; start when the first one arrives).

## What not to post

- Pure self-promotion ("just published a new piece, link below"). Has to add value in the body.
- Personal updates unrelated to the work. Different account, different audience.
- Hot takes on AI, tech industry layoffs, or political news. Off-brand and dilutes positioning.
- Reposts of other people's content with one-line commentary. Does not work on LinkedIn.
- Anything that mentions LinkedIn itself ("the algorithm did X", "LinkedIn is broken"). Insider baseball, audience does not care.

## Profile maintenance

The LinkedIn profile is the conversion surface. Once a post drives a profile visit, the profile has to do the closing work.

**Profile elements that matter:**

- Headline: should mention the project. Current draft suggestion: "Building Citizen of Nowhere | Global Metro Power Rankings | [marketing tech / analytics positioning]"
- About section: opens with what the project is, why it exists, and what kind of conversation the author is open to (consulting, speaking, collaboration).
- Featured section: pin the highest-performing Substack post and the methodology page link.
- Activity tab: should be 80 percent project-related posts, 20 percent thoughtful comments on adjacent voices' posts.

**Cadence for profile updates:** review monthly. Update headline if the project's positioning shifts. Update Featured if a new piece outperforms the pinned one.
