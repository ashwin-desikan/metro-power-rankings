Operating rules for the Citizen of Nowhere Facebook page. Internal document. NOTE: /docs/ became TRACKED on 2026-08-07; only docs/lens/ is still gitignored. The goal is friends-and-family broadcast: people in your existing personal network see what you are working on, occasionally engage, occasionally share. This is not a primary distribution channel and should not compete for time with Reddit, LinkedIn, or Substack. The point is presence and proof, not reach.

Last reviewed: 2026-05-02.

---

## Strategic frame

Facebook organic reach for analytical long-form content is poor in 2026 and getting worse. Pages typically see 1-5 percent of followers per post unless content goes viral or paid promotion is used. Meta deprioritizes outbound links, which directly conflicts with the "drive readers to Substack" goal that Reddit and LinkedIn serve.

Friends-and-family changes the calculation. Your network already knows you, already wants to support you, and is plugged into Facebook for reasons unrelated to discovering new newsletters. They will see your posts when they show up because the algorithm weights friends-of-page heavily. Your sister-in-law is going to see this. A LinkedIn post of the same content reaches a much larger audience but a much colder one.

The implicit upside: when one of your contacts mentions to a journalist or a podcaster that "my friend Ashwin runs this fascinating cities project," the Facebook page is what they Google. Having a real, populated page makes that conversation convert. An empty or absent page makes it die.

## Posting cadence

| Surface | Frequency | What goes here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substack long-form | 1 per week (Tuesdays) | Primary writing |
| Facebook | 1-2 per week | One piece-share + one curiosity hook OR did-you-know stat |

The critical rule: **do not post on Facebook on a schedule that requires writing original Facebook content**. Every Facebook post should be derivative of something else: a Substack piece, a metro page, a badge, a digest finding. If you find yourself writing a Facebook post from scratch, you are spending the wrong currency for this channel.

Best windows for friends-and-family in North America: **weekday mornings (8-10am ET) or Sunday mid-morning (10am-12pm ET)**. Weekends underperform on most pages but the friends-and-family case inverts this: people scroll Facebook on weekends, and your network is your audience.

## Repurposing rules

How to take existing content from Substack, the site, or the Insight Engine digest and adapt it for Facebook without writing new content.

### From a published Substack post

Within 24 hours of publish:

1. Pick the most compelling chart or OG card from the post (already generated at `public/share-cards/[piece]/`).
2. Take the first 2-3 sentences of the Substack post and tighten to one Facebook paragraph.
3. Add the Substack URL on its own line at the bottom.
4. Caption format: `[hook sentence]\n\n[one paragraph of context]\n\n[URL]`

### From a metro page

Single metro shares work well as "did you know" posts:

1. Pick a metro that has an interesting story (an outlier, a contested call, a known reader interest).
2. Find one or two genuinely surprising data points from the page.
3. Write 2-3 sentences. Link to `/rankings/[slug]`.

### From a badge page

Badge pages are themselves quotable lists:

1. Lead with the most counterintuitive entry on the badge.
2. Reference the badge URL.
3. Caption format: `[surprise hook]. The full list of [N] cities like this is at [URL].`

### From a digest entry

Anomaly digest entries that did not become full posts can become Facebook one-liners:

1. Pick the entry with the best story angle from `digests/YYYY-MM-DD.json`.
2. State the finding plainly. Link to the metro page.
3. Two sentences max. Save the analysis for the Substack version when you write it.

## What not to post

- Long-form text without an image. Facebook hides long text walls until clicked, and the click rate from friends-and-family is fine but not great. Always include an image.
- Pure self-promotion ("subscribe to my Substack") without any value in the body. Save the soft subscribe call for once a month.
- Reactions to news, politics, or industry drama. Off-brand for this project, dilutes the data-and-cities focus.
- Personal life updates. Different account, different audience. Friends-and-family is reading the page for the project, not for what you had for breakfast.
- Anything that mentions the algorithm or the platform itself.

## Decision gates

Review at 30 days, 90 days, and 180 days.

**At 30 days:**
- Is the page being maintained at the 1-2 posts per week target? If not, drop to 1 per week or pause without guilt.
- Are friends and family engaging at all? Even 1-2 reactions per post is fine for this channel.

**At 90 days:**
- Has any Facebook share led to inbound contact (a journalist, a friend-of-friend introduction, a podcast invite)? If yes, the channel is converting and worth maintaining.
- If zero inbound, consider dropping to monthly posts and keeping the page only as a brand-presence destination.

**At 180 days:**
- Total subscribers attributable to Facebook (via UTM in the Substack signup flow or Substack's own source attribution).
- If less than 20 subscribers attributed: the channel is not earning its keep. Maintain at minimum cadence (1 post per month) for brand presence, redirect time to higher-leverage channels.
- If 50+ subscribers attributed: surprising; double the cadence and treat as a real channel.
