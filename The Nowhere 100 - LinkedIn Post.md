Every "most powerful people" list is the same thing.

A magazine editor's guess. Reshuffled once a year. Defended by nobody.

So I built the opposite.

The Nowhere 100: a ranking of the hundred most powerful people alive, where every name is scored by the actual place or institution behind them, then linked back to it on a map.

Power isn't free-floating. It sits somewhere.

A president is only as powerful as the state he commands. A CEO as the company he steers. A striker as the club that pays him.

Give power an address and the order gets interesting:

→ BlackRock's Larry Fink outranks the Chancellor of Germany. Capital over cabinet.
→ The Pope sits 14th. Reach is power.
→ A single US state governor outranks most of the G7.
→ Roger Goodell makes the cut. The NFL is a power base.
→ Kim Jong-un lands in the 30s. A bomb, but a small economy. That seam was the hardest call in the model.

It spans fifteen kinds of power: heads of state, central bankers, judges, financiers, media owners, athletes, popes, kings, billionaires. A toggle hides the soft categories if you only trust office and money.

No black box. Published methodology. A snapshot date on every version. It rebuilds itself every week from live data, in one automated pass, with no manual step.

I would rather be transparently wrong and improvable than confidently vague.

Find the placement that makes you angry. Then follow the link to the city behind the person, and see if the anger survives the map.

List, methodology and the full write-up in the comments.

Who is ranked too high?
